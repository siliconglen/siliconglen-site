## Plans do not deliver themselves

Complex programmes have to connect decisions, people, suppliers, dependencies and changing evidence. When those parts drift apart, more reporting does not necessarily create more control.

I bring critical thinking into delivery: clarify the outcome, expose the constraints and coordinate the work needed to produce a result.

## What we can cover

- Clarifying outcomes, scope, ownership and measures of success.
- Programme and project planning grounded in real dependencies.
- Coordinating teams, stakeholders and multiple suppliers.
- Identifying delivery risks, assumptions and blocked decisions.
- Recovering work that is under pressure or has lost direction.
- Using feedback to adapt the approach while protecting the outcome.

## How the work happens

The engagement may be a focused diagnostic and recovery plan, interim hands-on leadership, or support through to an agreed outcome.

I agree scope, working pattern, governance and decision authority up front to fit the scale and urgency.

### Understand the situation
We establish what must be delivered, what has already been decided and where reality differs from the plan.

### Make the work visible
Outcomes, responsibilities, dependencies, constraints and decisions are made explicit.

### Coordinate delivery
I work with the people and suppliers involved to turn priorities into action and address obstacles.

### Learn and adapt
Evidence from delivery informs the next decision rather than being hidden by the original plan.

## When this is useful

- A complex programme needs experienced coordination.
- Multiple teams or suppliers must work towards one outcome.
- Delivery is moving but priorities or ownership remain unclear.
- A programme needs recovery, decisive action or a credible route forward.
- Organisational change must become practical delivery.

## When this isn't the right fit

This is not the right fit if you only need a report or recommendations without hands-on responsibility for moving delivery forward. If the immediate need is to examine a decision before implementation rather than lead the resulting programme, a Decision Support Workshop is likely to be more useful.
