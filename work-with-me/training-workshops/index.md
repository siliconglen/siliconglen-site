## Better thinking must be usable in real work

Critical thinking is most valuable when people can apply it to the decisions, plans and evidence in front of them. Training should therefore do more than describe concepts: it should help a team practise constructive challenge and carry useful methods back into work.

## What we can cover

- Framing the real problem before trying to solve it.
- Separating evidence, assumptions and interpretation.
- Finding blind spots and alternative explanations.
- Comparing options against explicit criteria.
- Challenging strategies and plans constructively.
- Recognising false confidence, hallucination and automation bias in AI output.
- Using AI without outsourcing human judgement.

## How the work happens

We agree the audience, context and decisions the learning should support. The session is then focused on relevant problems and practical participation rather than a generic method catalogue. Workshops can create space for a team to examine a live question while developing shared ways to think about future ones.

## What you leave with

Participants leave with shared language, practical approaches for questioning information and a clearer way to apply challenge to decisions and plans. A live workshop may also produce framed questions, tested assumptions, decision criteria or agreed next actions.

## When this is useful

- A team wants to become better at challenging assumptions.
- Leaders need more consistent decision quality.
- Constructive disagreement is difficult or evidence is interpreted inconsistently.
- People are using AI-generated material without a reliable verification habit.
- An organisation wants capability it can continue using itself.

## Build practical thinking capability

Describe who the session is for, the problems they face and what should be different afterwards.
