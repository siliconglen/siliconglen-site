## Knowledge Canvas {#knowledge-canvas}

<iframe class="media-embed" width="100%" height="450" src="https://www.youtube-nocookie.com/embed/oU1xIyOasCo" title="Knowledge Canvas" loading="lazy" allowfullscreen></iframe>

Given as part of Agile20Reflect (agile20reflect.org), this talk introduces Knowledge Canvas, a mind-map alternative for organising training and learning content to tell a story and help people learn and revise it. A digital-friendly format that also works alongside online video, designed with particular emphasis on people who prefer a clear structure. The approach has received strong positive feedback and has been used by a training company.
{: .lede}

I developed it to make talks and conferences more inclusive for neurodivergent audiences: approximately one in seven people in the UK have a neurodifference, according to [Acas](https://www.acas.org.uk/neurodiversity-at-work). I released it under a [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International licence](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/).

I first presented it at Agile Tour London in October 2020 and at Agile Lithuania on 7 September 2022. The Agile Lithuania organisers invited me to present early so other speakers could use the method while preparing their talks.

## Strategy Maps: connect your roadmaps to the bigger picture {#strategy-maps-agile-india-2022}

> "Most strategy dialogues end up with executives talking at cross-purposes because… nobody knows exactly what is meant by vision and strategy, and no two people ever quite agree on which topics belong where." — Geoffrey Moore, author and business adviser, Geoffrey Moore Consulting, *Escape Velocity*

All too often, strategy is a static PowerPoint that goes out of date and gives little indication of the "why of movement."

This talk shows how to form an agile strategy and visualise it in a complex landscape, connecting it to roadmaps, backlogs, Wardley mapping and the Strategy Cycle, drawing on work by Simon Wardley, Roman Pichler and Dave Snowden. It also uses a novel visualisation technique to help visualise the talk itself.
{: .lede}
